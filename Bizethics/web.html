    Moms take care of us. Let’s take care of them.

    Caregiving is the work that holds every family together, yet it is rarely seen, paid or thanked.

    Dear Mom: write to her

    For our lecturer: All of these reports are part of our Social Mini Project, so please go through all of the content.

    Executive summary

    Care work deserves to be seen, valued and paid

    “In collaboration with the VinUniversity Student Council, to treasure the ones who care for others.”
    How we work with the Student Council →

    Caregivers are overlooked. Their work goes unseen, unpaid, and unacknowledged. Caregiving is essential, but it should not mean that a woman has to put aside her well-being or identity.

    We first went to K Hospital to help sick children. What we found was a bigger, quieter problem: the care work of mothers, unpaid and unseen, which is at its most extreme there but exists in every home.

    We can either keep helping one ward at a time, or change how society treats carers everywhere. We chose to do both.

    The problemCarers, mostly mothers, do essential care work that is neglected, unpaid and unrecognised.
    What we doA social media campaign that changes how young people see their mothers’ care, and funding for the mothers at K Hospital who carry its heaviest form.
    What you can doDo one Care for the Carers act for your own mother this week: thank her, take over a task, or ask about her as a person. Then support the K Hospital mothers.
    Track A · Main

    Social media campaign: Care for the Carers

    Goal
    Remind the public that caregiving is real, demanding work that deserves recognition.
    Long-term aim
    Shift social attitudes at a systemic level, especially how children learn to view and treat their mothers.
    Track B · Supporting

    Mothers at K Hospital

    Who
    Mothers caring for patients at K Hospital (Vietnam National Cancer Hospital). They are among the most severely affected caregivers.
    Why this group
    They have the greatest need for support, so they serve as the representative group for funding. They are not the core problem; they are a concrete case that illustrates it.
    ⇄

    How the two tracks work together. K Hospital is live proof: it shows that these mothers can be supported, paid, and helped. Social media is systemic change: it changes how people perceive caregiving, which in turn changes how the public views the work at K Hospital. The hospital project gives the campaign credibility, and the campaign builds the awareness that makes the hospital project matter to more people.

    Love Behind the Ward is a social impact project by five VinUniversity students for the MANA3021 Business Ethics course.
