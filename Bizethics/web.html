    Executive summary

    Care work deserves to be visible, valued and fairly supported

    Caregiving is the work that holds families together, yet it is often invisible and insufficiently recognised. Care for the Carers addresses both the everyday attitudes that make care work invisible and the practical needs of caregivers in particularly demanding circumstances.

    We first went to K Hospital to help sick children. What we found was a bigger, quieter problem: the care work of mothers, unpaid and unseen, which is at its most extreme there but exists in every home.

    We can either keep helping one ward at a time, or change how society treats carers everywhere. We chose to do both.

    1The problemCare work, mostly done by mothers, is treated as an expected responsibility rather than meaningful work deserving recognition, shared responsibility and fair support.
    2What we doDear Mom, a social media campaign for children and young people, alongside practical support for caregivers at K Hospital Tan Trieu.
    3What you can doDo one Care for the Carers act for your own mother this week: thank her, take over a task, or ask about her as a person. Then support the K Hospital mothers.

    How it works

    Two tracks that support each other

    👆 Tap a track to open it.

    Track A · Main

    Dear Mom: a social media campaign

    +Tap to see the goal and long-term aim
    Goal
    Encourage children and young people to recognise their own mothers’ caregiving as real work, express appreciation, share household and care responsibilities, and pay attention to their mothers’ needs and personal aspirations.
    Long-term aim
    Make care work more visible, valued, and fairly supported in everyday life, by challenging the attitudes that can make it invisible.
    Track B · Supporting

    Practical support at K Hospital Tan Trieu

    +Tap to see who we support and why
    Who
    Caregivers at K Hospital Tan Trieu, mostly mothers caring for children undergoing cancer treatment.
    Why this group
    The pressures of caring for children through cancer treatment make the need for support particularly visible. Their circumstances make them a relevant group for practical support, but the underlying problem extends beyond the hospital.
    What we did
    Contributions to a local volunteer group, food distribution, and flowers.
    ⇄

    How the two tracks work together. The hospital activity gives the campaign a concrete example of caregivers’ circumstances, while the campaign seeks to broaden public understanding of the issue beyond the hospital.

    At K Hospital

    A volunteer group near K Hospital Tân Triều

    Run by two women, Ms. Thu and Ms. Thanh. The group prepares fresh meals and provides essential supplies for hospital families, handing out food near the hospital four to five times a week.

    We joined them on 17 October 2026: we contributed VND 2,000,000 and helped distribute nearly 500 meal portions.
