    A wall of faces, each with a letter to Mom

    Make a little face that looks like you, write the thing you have always wanted to tell your mom, and pin it to the wall. Tap any face to read their letter.

    We went first

    Why don’t we say it?

    In many Vietnamese families, love is not spoken. It is the fruit cut and left on your desk, the “ăn cơm chưa?” on the phone, the jacket she makes you take. So saying “con thương mẹ” out loud can feel strange, even a little embarrassing.

    That does not make saying it wrong. It only means we have not practised it.

    The words do not replace what she does; they tell her that we finally see it.

    For 20/10, our team lead Thảo Linh wrote this letter to her mom. Even after years of being praised for her writing, it was one of the very few times her mom had read something she wrote. Her mom cried.

    “This was the first time I ever dared to tell my mom how much I love her.”Thảo Linh

    “I miss you more than I remember you.”
    Ocean Vuong, quoted in the letter

    Research

    Children see “mom”, not the person

    This is the insight our campaign targets. Behind every face on this wall is a child who stopped for a minute to look at their mother as a person, and at her care as real work.

    “Since when did her name not belong to herself anymore?”There is a name for this process: matrescence, the process of becoming a mother, first proposed by medical anthropologist Dana Raphael and developed by Aurelie Athan as “a deep identity change, not just postpartum depression”.Raphael, 1970; Athan & Bastidas, 2021
    Two in three mothers felt they had lost part of themselvesIn a survey of 2,000 British mothers, two-thirds said they felt they have lost part of their identity.PRNewswire, 2023
    “Mẹ nuôi con biển hồ lai láng, con nuôi mẹ kể tháng kể ngày”A mother raises her child with an ocean-like abundance; the child raises the mother counting months and counting days. A custom formed through generations, in which receiving care back is only an option for the child.Vietnamese proverb
